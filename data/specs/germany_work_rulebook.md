# German Employment & Labor Compliance Rulebook (Arbeitsrecht Kompendium)

## 1. German Working Time Act (Arbeitszeitgesetz - ArbZG)

### § 1 ArbZG - Purpose of the Act
The purpose of this Act is to guarantee safety and health protection of employees in the organization of working time, to improve framework conditions for flexible working hours, and to protect Sundays and state-recognized public holidays as days of work rest and mental recovery.

### § 3 ArbZG - Daily Working Hours
The statutory daily working time of employees may not exceed eight hours. It may only be extended to up to ten hours if an average of eight hours per working day is not exceeded within six calendar months or within 24 weeks.

### § 4 ArbZG - Mandatory Rest Breaks
Work must be interrupted by pre-scheduled rest breaks:
- At least 30 minutes for a total working time of more than six to nine hours.
- At least 45 minutes for a total working time of more than nine hours.
- Rest breaks under § 4 ArbZG may be divided into intervals of at least 15 minutes each. Working without a break for more than six consecutive hours is strictly unlawful.

| Total Daily Shift Duration | Minimum Statutory Break | Minimum Permissible Break Sub-Interval | Maximum Consecutive Work Without Break |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Up to 6 hours | 0 minutes | N/A | 6 hours |
| > 6 hours up to 9 hours | 30 minutes | 15 minutes | 6 hours |
| > 9 hours (up to 10 hours) | 45 minutes | 15 minutes | 6 hours |

### § 5 ArbZG - Daily Rest Periods
Employees must be granted an uninterrupted rest period of at least 11 hours following the end of daily working hours. In hospitals, care facilities, hospitality, and transport enterprises, the rest period may be reduced by up to one hour if compensated within one calendar month.

## 2. Dismissal Protection Act (Kündigungsschutzgesetz - KSchG)

### § 1 KSchG - Social Justification of Terminations
Termination of an employment relationship by an employer is socially unjustified and legally ineffective if it is not conditioned on:
1. Reasons related to the person or character of the employee (Personenbedingte Kündigung).
2. Reasons related to the conduct of the employee (Verhaltensbedingte Kündigung).
3. Urgent operational enterprise requirements precluding continued employment (Betriebsbedingte Kündigung).

### § 14 KSchG - Threshold of Application & Employee Headcount Weighting
The provisions of § 1 through § 13 KSchG apply only to enterprises that regularly employ more than 10 full-time equivalent (FTE) employees (excluding trainees/apprentices). For employees hired before December 31, 2003, the former threshold of 5 employees applies under grandfathering rules.

When calculating the regular enterprise threshold under § 14 KSchG, part-time employees are factored according to the following statutory weighting matrix:

| Weekly Working Hours | Calculation Weighting Factor (FTE Equivalent) | Statutory Basis |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Up to 20 hours per week | 0.50 | § 23 Abs. 1 Satz 4 KSchG |
| More than 20 to 30 hours per week | 0.75 | § 23 Abs. 1 Satz 4 KSchG |
| More than 30 hours per week | 1.00 | § 23 Abs. 1 Satz 4 KSchG |
| Trainees / Apprentices (Auszubildende) | 0.00 (Excluded from headcount) | § 23 Abs. 1 Satz 4 KSchG |

## 3. Federal Vacation Act (Bundesurlaubsgesetz - BUrlG)

### § 3 BUrlG - Statutory Minimum Annual Leave
The statutory annual paid leave is at least 24 working days based on a standard 6-day working week. In practice, for standard enterprise working weeks, statutory leave scales as follows:

| Working Days Per Week | Statutory Minimum Vacation Days Per Calendar Year | Act Reference |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 6 days per week | 24 working days | § 3 Abs. 1 BUrlG |
| 5 days per week | 20 working days | § 3 Abs. 1 BUrlG (standardized) |
| 4 days per week | 16 working days | § 3 Abs. 1 BUrlG (pro-rata) |
| 3 days per week | 12 working days | § 3 Abs. 1 BUrlG (pro-rata) |

### § 4 BUrlG - Waiting Period for Full Vacation Entitlement
The full statutory annual leave entitlement is acquired for the first time only after the employment relationship has existed continuously for six months (Wartezeit).
